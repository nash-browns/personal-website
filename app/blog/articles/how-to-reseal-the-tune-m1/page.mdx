## Finding the Leak
I was washing my truck one day when I noticed that the wooden shelf that holds my microwave and battery was looking damp — and starting to grow a little mold. When I went looking for how it was getting wet, I found a slow leak coming through one of the bed corners. Funny enough, it only leaked at the car wash; it didn't seem to happen when it was raining.
<CenteredImage image={moldyShelf} altText={'Mold growing on the wooden shelf that holds the microwave and battery'} title={'The Moldy Shelf'} width={500} height={500} />

## Bed Corner Reseal
Toyota Tacoma truck beds have a small gap where the back of the bed and the sides meet. This was great for me, because I used the gap to run all of the external wiring into the camper, saving me from having to drill any holes into my truck! The bad news is: these gaps can leak. Over time, mine did.
<CenteredImage image={bedGap} altText={'The gap in the Tacoma bed corner where the wiring runs into the camper'} title={'The Bed Corner Gap'} width={500} height={500} />

<YoutubeAd videoID={'nE98ZA_9Rws'} description={'Resealing the Tune M1'} />

The process is easy, and the camper stays on the truck the whole time. After you have the [reseal putty][1], all you need to do is:
1. Open up the package (the putty is kind of gross)
2. Scrape off any old, crumbly putty from the camper
3. Reapply the new putty

<CenteredImage image={puddy} altText={'A fresh package of the gray reseal putty'} title={'The Putty'} width={500} height={500} />

This job took 30 minutes. I applied the [putty][1] and voila, the camper no longer leaks when the hose is pointed at it!

I used barely any of the brick. I think I could reseal the camper 10 times with what is left. The rest went into a Ziploc bag, and hopefully it stays pliable until the next reseal.
<TwoCenteredImages image={[puddyOutside, puddyOutside2]} altText={['Putty pressed into the bed corner from outside', 'The resealed corner from another angle']} titles={['Putty Applied', 'Sealed Corner']} width={500} height={500} />

## Front Weather Sealing
I also had a weather sealing issue. It looks like the glue that held the seal to the camper just got old. It doesn't leak at this point, so I am not going to touch it, but if you are looking to replace your seals, here is what Tune uses: a [double D-bulb gasket][3] on the sides, and on the front, almost always a [single 1" D-bulb][2].
<CenteredImage image={tuneWeatherSeal} altText={'The front weather seal where the adhesive is starting to let go'} title={'Front Weather Seal'} width={500} height={500} />

## Final Thoughts
All in all, I am really happy with the weather resistance the Tune provides. I have never had a wet night. If this is all the maintenance that is needed, I am a happy camper!
